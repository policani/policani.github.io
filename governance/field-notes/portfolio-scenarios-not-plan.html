A portfolio needs scenarios, not a single annual plan

TL;DR

One annual plan stops describing reality the moment capacity, demand, or assumptions change. Keep a few workable versions of the portfolio instead. Agree in advance what you would protect, delay, reshape, or stop.

What the paper develops

A single annual plan gets less useful the moment capacity, dependencies, demand, or assumptions shift. Every plan eventually meets a surprise. What matters is what happens next. Most portfolios respond by keeping up appearances. Everything stays alive, delay spreads evenly, and the plan stays formally in force long after it stopped describing anything. This paper prepares the options in advance instead, with triggers and regular reviews, so leaders can move between them deliberately.

What to do next

Keep a small set of workable portfolio shapes that show capacity, dependencies, and tradeoffs. Agree the triggers ahead of time. When conditions change, leaders should be choosing between options they already prepared, not improvising around a frozen plan.
