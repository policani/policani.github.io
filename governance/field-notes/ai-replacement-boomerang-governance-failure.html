Your people are why the tool looks ready

TL;DR

When an AI tool drafts work your staff review and send, the trial you run before reducing roles counts the tool and your people's fixes together, and those fixes often leave no record. Reducing the roles removes the fixes, so the tool's own error rate shows only after the people catching its mistakes are gone. Before you decide, save each draft before anyone edits it, compare it with what was sent, and have someone who did not edit the drafts judge a sample against pass criteria written down first.

What the paper develops

The decision

An AI tool now drafts work your staff used to write. They review each draft, fix what needs fixing and send it. After a few months the error and complaint figures look good, and the question is whether you still need as many people in those roles.

What the trial counts

Older automation passed the cases it could not handle to a person and counted the handoff. A drafting tool hands every case to a person, and an edit made in place often leaves no record. So the trial measures the tool and your people's fixes together, and a quality sample of finished work checks the work after the fixes.

Skilled people fix problems quietly. In a study of 26 nurses at nine hospitals, nurses dealt with 93 percent of the problems they met on the spot and told no one who could remove the cause, so the record showed work running smoothly [1].

Why reducing and watching fails

Reducing the roles removes the fixes. For a cut, the tool's own error rate appears when undoing the cut is costly. For attrition or a hiring pause, it appears late and in pieces, after errors and unusual cases have built up.

What to do before deciding

Save each draft before anyone edits it and compare it with what was sent, recording how long each took to review and fix. A staff log is not enough: in an experiment with 758 consultants, wrong answers written with AI were rated more persuasive than wrong answers written without it, and the errors showed only against a problem with one correct answer [3].

Write down the pass criteria, how cases will be sampled and the decision date before anyone's job depends on the result. Have someone who did not edit the drafts judge a sample. Where review would end, the raw drafts must meet the standard; where reviewers would be fewer, those who remain must have time to read every draft and make the fixes.

What to do next

Save every draft before staff edit it, and judge a sample against pass criteria set in advance, before reducing any roles.
