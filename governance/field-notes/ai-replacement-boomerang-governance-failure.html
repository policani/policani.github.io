The AI replacement boomerang is a governance failure

TL;DR

Cutting people because AI can do part of their work looks like immediate value. If nobody redesigned the work itself, the cost comes back as rework, lost know-how, and a capability pipeline you cannot quickly rehire.

What the paper develops

Cutting headcount can look like immediate AI value while the underlying work stays exactly as it was. This paper traces how that shortcut comes back: rework, lost operating knowledge, brittle exception handling, and a damaged pipeline of people who can do the job. It treats the cut as a portfolio decision. Redesign the work first. Show the new way can carry the ordinary cases and the hard ones. Keep the human capability you are still going to need.

What to do next

Do not book the savings until the work, the exception path, the handover of knowledge, and the way people learn the job have all been redesigned. Removing people without redesigning the system moves cost into rework and fragility.
