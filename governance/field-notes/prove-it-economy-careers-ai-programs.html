A team's AI success says the least about the person you were surest of

TL;DR

When an AI program succeeds and no one can see who did what, even an honest sponsor tends to split the credit by what they already believed about each person. A team success can then seem to confirm that view. It lifts the whole team, and most the people already rated highest. But a result tells you something new about a person only where it differs from what you expected. So it should change your view of the person you were surest of the least. Credit each person only for the parts you can point to, and credit the rest to the team equally.

What the paper develops

The decision

An AI program you sponsor has delivered, and many people worked on it. What the result says about each of them will feed recognition, promotions and who leads the next program. The usual approach is to discount the loudest claims and ask several people what happened. But people recall more of their own part than of others', "in the absence of deliberate deceit" [1]. Their answers also shift with how the question is asked [1]. Someone still has to divide the result.

What fills the gap you cannot see

In experiments, judges who saw each person's own score treated men and women alike. Judges who saw only a pair's total split it by what they already believed [2]. In economics, author order does not show who did what. There, a paper written with others raised men's tenure chances by 7.4 points and women's by 4.7 [2]. When a joint paper was withdrawn and no one could tell which author was at fault, well-known coauthors saw "modest if any decline" in citations [3]. Carrying these patterns to AI programs is this paper's reasoning.

Why your view of the person you were surest of should move least

A result tells you something new about a person only to the extent it differs from what you expected of them. Jin and his colleagues, in a model of blame, note that a tight prior means new events have little further effect [3]. For the person you were surest of, the success was mostly expected. So it should change your view of them the least. Yet the usual division gives them the most, as the studies show for people judges already favor. In this paper's judgment, holding back is the cheaper mistake, because over-crediting shapes the next split and joint results cannot reveal it.

What to change

Before results are known, have the pieces of the work written down as they are done, and confirmed by the people named. Read that record before you form a view. Credit each person in full for the parts it shows and for what you saw them do. Credit the rest to the team, equally to every member. Raise no one's standing beyond that. No study here shows that a record changes how a judge divides credit; the closest test failed [1]. That step is this paper's judgment.
