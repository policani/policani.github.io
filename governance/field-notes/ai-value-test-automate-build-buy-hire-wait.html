AI operating governance · Field note

An AI build that beats today's price may not beat next year's

TL;DR

A business case for an AI build or a long model deal usually shows its saving against what a vendor charges today. But a vendor's model generally keeps its launch price. What falls is the price of doing a task at a set level, because newer models reach that level for less. So today's price held flat assumes the business never moves to a cheaper model. Judge a build whose running cost is committed, or a deal that pins a model, against the bill of a buyer who moves at a realistic pace. And show how fast prices would have to fall to wipe out the saving.

What the paper develops

The decision

A team asks to fund an AI build that cuts running cost. Or a vendor offers a discount for a multi-year deal that fixes one model and its price per token. The case shows the saving against what a vendor charges today. From April 2024 to November 2025, the price of reaching a fixed test score fell about 5 to 10 times a year for the best-value models. Over the same period, the cost of running the best model rose about 3 to 18 times a year [1]. That is how AI gets cheaper while bills rise.

Why the price you pay stays put

Closed models generally keep their launch price, and new versions often enter at about the same price [2]. The fall reaches a buyer only by moving to a newer, cheaper model that does the task well enough. Each move costs testing and rework. Buyers do not always move for price. On one large cloud, most firms put more than 90 percent of their use on one model [2].

What that does to a build's saving

As an illustration, take a build that halves today's running cost for three years. Say the price of doing the task falls about 1.7 times a year, and the business moves at no cost as soon as a cheaper model appears. Then the build's whole saving is gone. With yearly moves, it takes a fall of about 2.7 times a year. On the one test tracked by level, the lowest level fell about 1.7 times a year and the top about 31 to 32 times [1]. Holding today's price flat is the forecast for a business that never moves.

What to put in the case

Name which price the case buys. Show the fall that wipes out the saving at three paces, with moving costs, beside the falls recorded at a similar level. For a deal, say what it fixes and for how long. For a build meant to lead, name the lead and check it at each review. A general model scored higher than a large custom finance model on four of five public tests. But small tuned models still led on narrow, structured tasks [3, 5].

What to do next

Make each AI cost case say which price it buys. Show the yearly price fall that would wipe out the saving if you never move, move once a year, or move as soon as a cheaper model meets the need, with the cost of each move counted, beside the falls recorded at a similar level.
