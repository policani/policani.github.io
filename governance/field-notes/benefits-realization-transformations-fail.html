Benefits realization is where transformations quietly fail

TL;DR

The program ships without missing a deadline anyone notices. The milestones are checked, the team is thanked, everyone moves on. Months later the value that justified the whole thing has not appeared, and nobody is sure anyone is still measuring.

What the paper develops

Most transformation failures are not dramatic. The program does not collapse or miss its deadline in a way anyone notices inside the company. It ships, and the value quietly never arrives. This paper explains how benefits lose their owner somewhere between approval, build, adoption, and everyday operation, and sets out a practical discipline: a named benefits owner, a credible baseline, early and late evidence, and a review that can change the approach when the promised result does not appear.

What to do next

Name someone who owns the benefit, agree the baseline before you start, and book the realization gate at approval rather than arranging it after go-live. A project is not finished when the output ships. It is finished when you can show the operating change.
