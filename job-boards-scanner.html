Role match, Scores jobs

Within each list, entries are alternatives: a job needs to match any one. The four lists add up to one score, and adding entries never removes a job. Strong matches go to Main, near-misses to Review, and weak matches are screened out.

How this works, Role match

Press Enter to add each entry. Pasting several lines adds one entry per line. Double-click an entry to edit it. Matching ignores capitalization and punctuation.

Primary titles. Use one title keyword or phrase per entry. Multi-word phrases can also match titles with a short word gap, such as AI Manager matching AI Program Manager. Quotation marks are not required.

Related titles. Use one related title word or phrase per entry. These broaden discovery when the exact title varies across employers, including short word gaps in title phrases. Very general words such as Strategy, Strategic, Initiatives, or Readiness add score but do not count as a title match on their own.

Seniority words. Use one seniority word or phrase per entry. These are checked against the job title, such as Director, Principal, Lead, or Senior Manager.

Body evidence. Use one keyword or phrase per entry. These are searched in the job description to prove the role contains work you care about, not just a matching title.

The job titles you are aiming for. A match is the strongest title signal and the usual route into Main. Phrases also match with a few words between, so AI Manager matches AI Program Manager.

    Broader title words for roles you would also consider. A match scores lower than a primary title: one usually goes to Review, while two related matches, or one plus two body-evidence matches, can reach Main.

      Levels you want, such as Director or Senior, checked in the job title. A match adds a small boost. Adding words only helps; it never removes a job.

        Phrases from job descriptions that show the work fits, such as capacity planning. Each match raises the score, and two or more help a job reach Main. Adding phrases never removes a job.

              How this works, Salary

              A job appears when its posted range reaches the selected salary amount, including ranges entirely above it. For example, a role listed at $160,000-$240,000 appears when this is set to $220,000.
